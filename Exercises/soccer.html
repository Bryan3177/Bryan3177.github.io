The World's Top Three Soccer Players

Lionel Messi

Lionel Messi is widely described as the greatest footballer of his generation, and for many supporters the greatest of all time. Born in Rosario, Argentina, in 1987, he was diagnosed with a growth hormone deficiency as a child, and his family moved to Spain when Barcelona agreed to pay for his treatment. He joined the club's famous La Masia academy at thirteen, made his first team debut at sixteen, and spent the next two decades rewriting the record books. With Barcelona he won ten La Liga titles and four Champions League trophies, scoring an astonishing 672 goals in 778 appearances, a club record that is unlikely ever to be approached.

What separates Messi from other prolific scorers is the completeness of his game. He is a left footed forward who can beat defenders in tight spaces, thread passes that no one else appears to see, and finish calmly from any angle. In the 2011-12 season he scored 91 goals in a single calendar year, a world record. He has won the Ballon d'Or a record eight times, along with six European Golden Shoes.

For many years the one gap in his career was a senior international trophy. That changed in 2021, when he captained Argentina to victory at the Copa America, and again in 2022, when he led his country to the World Cup in Qatar, scoring twice in a dramatic final against France and being named the tournament's best player. After a spell at Paris Saint-Germain he moved to Inter Miami in Major League Soccer in 2023, where his arrival transformed the profile of the league in North America.

Cristiano Ronaldo

Cristiano Ronaldo is the most prolific goalscorer in the recorded history of the sport, and the athlete against whom Messi has been measured for nearly twenty years. He was born on the Portuguese island of Madeira in 1985, joined Sporting CP as a teenager, and was signed by Manchester United in 2003 after a friendly in which he tormented the United defence. Under Alex Ferguson he developed from a flashy winger into a ruthless forward, winning three Premier League titles and his first Champions League in 2008.

His move to Real Madrid in 2009 for a then world record fee began the most productive period of his career. In nine seasons he scored 450 goals in 438 games and won four more Champions League titles, including three in a row between 2016 and 2018. He later played for Juventus in Italy, returned briefly to Manchester United, and then joined Al Nassr in Saudi Arabia, a transfer that dramatically raised the international profile of the Saudi Pro League.

Ronaldo's game is built on physical power, relentless training and an unusual versatility in front of goal. He is equally dangerous with either foot and is one of the finest headers of a ball the sport has seen, capable of hanging in the air long enough to pick his spot. He has won five Ballon d'Or awards and holds the record for most international goals in men's football, having passed 130 for Portugal. His greatest moment in national colours came at Euro 2016, when Portugal won the tournament despite his early injury in the final.

Kylian Mbappe

Kylian Mbappe is the player most often named as the heir to Messi and Ronaldo, and at the time of writing he is the most feared attacker in world football. Born in the Paris suburb of Bondy in 1998, he came through the academy at Monaco and broke into the first team at sixteen. In the 2016-17 season he helped Monaco win Ligue 1 and reach the semi finals of the Champions League, announcing himself with a series of performances that made him the most sought after teenager in Europe.

Paris Saint-Germain signed him in 2017 in a deal worth around 180 million euros, the second most expensive transfer ever completed. Over seven seasons in Paris he became the club's all time leading scorer, passing 250 goals and winning six league titles. In 2024 he joined Real Madrid, completing a move that had been rumoured for years.

His defining quality is speed. He has been timed at well over thirty-five kilometres per hour while carrying the ball, and few defenders can recover once he is running at them. That pace is combined with excellent close control and a cold, efficient finish. He became a World Cup winner with France in 2018 at the age of nineteen, scoring in the final against Croatia, and four years later he scored a hat-trick in the 2022 final against Argentina, the first player to do so in a World Cup final since 1966. He finished that tournament as the leading scorer and has since become captain of the French national team.
