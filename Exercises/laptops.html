Three Top-Selling Laptop Computers

Apple MacBook Air

The Apple MacBook Air is the best selling premium laptop in the world, and for many buyers it is the default choice when they want a thin, light machine that simply works. The current models are built around Apple's own M series processors, which combine the processor, graphics and memory on a single chip. The practical result is a laptop that runs silently, because there is no cooling fan at all, and that regularly delivers fifteen to eighteen hours of real world battery life.

The design is instantly recognisable. The all aluminium body weighs around 1.24 kilograms and is roughly one centimetre thick, yet it feels completely rigid. The Liquid Retina display is sharp and bright, the keyboard is comfortable for long typing sessions, and the large glass trackpad is still widely considered the best on any laptop. Two Thunderbolt ports and a MagSafe charging connector handle connectivity, along with a headphone jack that supports high impedance headphones.

Where the Air makes compromises is expansion and raw sustained power. Memory and storage are soldered to the board, so the configuration you buy is the configuration you keep, and the entry level model can feel tight for heavy video work. Because there is no fan, very long exports will eventually slow down as the chip throttles. For everyday tasks, however, it is exceptionally fast, waking from sleep instantly and handling dozens of browser tabs, office documents and photo editing without complaint.

It is particularly popular with students and business users, helped by education pricing and strong resale value. Full specifications are available on the Apple website.

Dell XPS 13

The Dell XPS 13 has been the leading Windows ultraportable for more than a decade, and it is the machine most often recommended to buyers who need Windows but want Apple levels of build quality. Its defining feature is the InfinityEdge display, a screen with borders so narrow that Dell was able to fit a thirteen inch panel into a chassis closer in size to an eleven inch laptop. Buyers can choose a bright full HD panel for maximum battery life or a sharper OLED option with deep blacks and excellent colour accuracy.

The construction mixes a machined aluminium lid and base with a carbon fibre or woven glass fibre palm rest, which stays cool and comfortable during long working sessions. Most configurations weigh around 1.2 kilograms. Inside, recent models use Intel Core Ultra processors, with up to 32GB of memory and fast solid state storage, which is enough for software development, spreadsheets and light creative work.

There are trade offs. To keep the profile slim Dell has moved almost entirely to Thunderbolt ports, so an adapter is usually needed for USB-A devices, HDMI monitors and SD cards. The webcam sits in a very small bezel and, although it has improved, it is not the strongest part of the package. Some recent revisions have also adopted a capacitive function row and a seamless trackpad, changes that divide opinion among reviewers.

Even so, the combination of a compact body, a good keyboard and a genuinely excellent screen keeps the XPS 13 at the top of most buying guides. Details are on the Dell website.

HP Pavilion 15

The HP Pavilion 15 represents the other end of the market, and in pure unit sales it outsells most premium machines. It is a mainstream fifteen inch laptop designed for families, home offices and students who need a capable computer without spending a great deal of money. Prices typically sit in the mid range, which makes it one of the most common laptops in retail stores and on high street shelves.

Configurations vary widely, and that flexibility is a large part of its appeal. Buyers can choose between Intel Core and AMD Ryzen processors, usually with 8GB or 16GB of memory and a solid state drive of 256GB to 1TB. The fifteen inch full HD screen is comfortable for spreadsheets and video, and unlike thinner rivals the Pavilion keeps a full sized keyboard with a number pad, which matters to anyone doing accounts or data entry.

Connectivity is generous. Most models include USB-A ports, USB-C, HDMI and a headphone jack, so older printers, monitors and drives work without adapters. Many versions also allow the memory and storage to be upgraded after purchase, which is increasingly rare and extends the useful life of the machine considerably.

The compromises are the ones you would expect at this price. The plastic and aluminium chassis has more flex than the MacBook Air or XPS 13, battery life is typically closer to seven or eight hours, and the display is dimmer with weaker colour coverage. For browsing, study, office work and video calls it is more than sufficient, and it remains one of the best value choices available. More information is on the HP website.
